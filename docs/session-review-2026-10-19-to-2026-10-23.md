## Confirmed software defects

### Holiday handling blocks NIFTY Setup Lab checks

October 20 was an NSE trading holiday for Dussehra, as listed in the
[official F&O holiday circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FAOP71777.pdf).
However, `scheduler/market_hours.py:is_market_day` accepts every weekday.
At October 21, 09:15 IST, `expected_closed_bar('15minute', now)` therefore
returns October 20, 15:15 instead of October 19, 15:15.

`nifty_lab/setups.py:technical_read` checks continuity across its recent candle
window. The nonexistent holiday candle becomes a false data gap. The recorded
decision timeline matches this explanation:

| Date | Recorded Lab decisions | Coverage |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| October 19 | 275 | All five setups checked |
| October 21 | 14 | Setup4 only, beginning 14:20 IST |
| October 22 | 75 | Setup4 only |
| October 23 | 187 | Setups1/2/3/5 resume at 12:00 IST; Setup4 throughout |

The five-minute gap ages out of the continuity window on Wednesday afternoon;
the fifteen-minute gap ages out on Friday at noon. This explains much of the
missing coverage. It does not prove the blocked signals would have won.
All five Lab trade rows were entered on October 19. They represent four unique
entry decisions: Setup1 technical twice, Setup2 technical plus its combined
counterpart, and Setup4 technical once.

Correction: use a versioned exchange-specific session calendar for scheduling,
expected bars and candle continuity. Test holiday reopenings, weekends, special
sessions and partial final candles. Never manufacture candles for closed days.
Do not apply the NSE day calendar to MCX evening sessions.

### Scalping quote checks compare different observation times

Of 133 scalping records, 127 carry a `quote_gap` interruption, four have missing
session exits, and only two are performance-eligible. Ninety quote-gap records
contain sufficient timestamp detail to reproduce a clock-comparison race.

In all ninety, the receipt timestamp is slightly later than the tick's captured
check-start timestamp, while the exchange quote is within the existing freshness
tolerance and the stream is connected. The receipt leads check-start by a median
65.7 milliseconds, at most 603.2 milliseconds. For example:

```text
Trade:       SC-C1D42F9755D0
Check start: 2026-10-23 14:34:13.850389 IST
Receipt:     2026-10-23 14:34:13.851088 IST
Difference:  0.000699 seconds
```

`scalping/service.py:tick` captures `now` before reading a concurrently updated
feed snapshot. `scalping/rules.py:fresh` rejects any negative receipt age.
The same ninety recorded timestamp pairs pass the unchanged three-second
freshness test when evaluated immediately after the receipt. The remaining
37 quote-gap flags lack detailed timestamps; their cause cannot be established.

Correction: copy a coherent quote snapshot and record the observation time after
that copy. Keep explicit replay timestamps deterministic. Preserve strict quote
age and synchronization limits; do not change historical receipt timestamps or
simply extend freshness tolerances. Do not automatically rehabilitate all old
trades: a genuine missed stop or missing exit still requires exclusion.

### Expiry spread selection receives the wrong data shape

The worker logged five occurrences of:

```text
cannot use 'dict' as a dict key (unhashable type: 'dict')
```

`expiry/feed.py` supplies CE and PE as quote dictionaries. However,
`expiry/rules.py:select_spread` calls `quotes.get(snapshot.get(side), {})`,
attempting to use that dictionary as a key. An actual archived expiry-day
snapshot reproduces the exception without network access.

The 600 expiry decisions include 224 outside-entry-window, 148 extended-signal,
225 no-signal, two premium-conflict and one signal-too-old results. Most checks
were legitimately rejected, but qualifying paths can crash at spread selection.
Zero completed trades is consequently not evidence that the expiry approaches
are ineffective.

Correction: make the feed/selector contract explicit, validate matching index,
expiry, option kind and lot size, and replay the recorded frames offline after
the fix. Add a test using the real feed shape, not only token-only fixtures.
Replaying this already-reviewed week is a reproduction test, not independent
strategy validation.

## Trading performance concerns

### Stops and repeated index entries need validation

Four BANKNIFTY trades all stopped out within seven to 44 seconds. Three had
initial risks of 22.45, 13.75 and 8.95 points, respectively,
or 0.179, 0.080 and 0.055 ATR; the fourth risked 31.70 points or 0.204 ATR.
These stops warrant a price-noise and slippage
review. The seven-second trade lost approximately 1.419 R at the observed exit.
Tight stops can amplify small price moves and costs, but widening them without
retesting can increase absolute losses.

Test structural stops against volatility floors, maintaining fixed cash risk by
reducing position size. Recalculate the target and reward/risk from the actual
entry observation. A starting research grid is no floor versus 0.25, 0.35 and
0.50 ATR; these are proposed test values, not approved live settings.

October 22 produced seven closed index entries and -4.619 gross R. Test a new
structure/retest requirement after a loss against the current repeat-entry rule.
Compare a higher-timeframe trend/slope filter and a trend-strength or range
filter separately. Do not combine many indicators before measuring each one's
incremental effect.

Thirteen closed index trades scored 95, but only two won. The score is a rule
confluence score, not a calibrated probability. Raising the minimum score is
not supported by these results.

### Scalping costs dominate the observed edge

There are 129 scalping rows with numeric exits and four with unknown exits.
The following is a diagnostic of the recorded marks, mostly excluded from
performance reporting, not a validated backtest:

| Numeric-exit observations | Wins | Win rate | Sum R | Profit factor |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gross | 65/129 | 50.39% | +26.408 | 1.404 |
| After stored cost model | 60/129 | 46.51% | -30.025 | 0.676 |

Modeled costs consume 56.433 R across those rows. Median round-trip cost is
0.424 R per trade; the 95th percentile is 0.745 R. Five gross winners become net
losers. A 50% gross win rate alone is therefore an inadequate acceptance test.

Research priorities are a larger executable move relative to spread and costs,
less noisy entry timeframes, and realistic option-contract bid/ask replay.
Do not lower the assumed costs just to improve the report: validate the model
against actual contract sizes, premiums, fees and observed spreads. Spot R
cannot establish net option profitability, especially near expiry.

Diagnostic examples worth retesting after monitoring is corrected are SENSEX
Scalp1 with premium confirmation (10 numeric exits, seven net winners, +3.097 R)
and SENSEX Scalp3 technical (three numeric exits, +3.514 R). All ten observations
in the former group are currently excluded; the latter has only one eligible
observation. These are research leads, not winning strategies or deployment
recommendations. Variants share signals and have different selection gates;
their unpaired win rates do not establish that a filter improves performance.

### Futures and VIX need reliable coverage before optimization

At the 19 index entries, futures context is ready for only four; six are
unavailable, four warming up, one stale and four missing. Ready context can
still be neutral. Keep futures confirmation in shadow mode until matching
time-aligned observations exist. Then compare the same base signals with and
without futures price/VWAP, OI, volume and spot-futures basis confirmation.
An OI change identifies positioning changes, not participant identity.

VIX was recorded at seven of 19 index entries and 24 of 133 scalp entries.
Coverage is insufficient to choose volatility bands reliably. An unavailable
value must remain unavailable rather than
be filled with today's VIX or a future observation.

Live/backtest parity also needs attention: NIFTY live `_stop_level` uses nearby
structure, whereas `_backtest_stop` uses ATR/fallback risk. A historical test
with different stops does not validate the current live stop implementation.

## Operations and alerts

All 36 recorded index alert deliveries are marked sent: 28 NIFTY and eight
BANKNIFTY. Median delivery latency is 0.76 seconds and maximum 8.70 seconds.
This archive does not show an index Telegram delivery failure.
All exported Lab and scalping trades have Telegram disabled.

Index entry detection delay has a median 16.2 seconds and maximum 74.8 seconds.
Scalping entries have a median 0.95 seconds and maximum 7.87 seconds. Do not
replace observed entry prices with earlier candle closes to make fills look
better; audit the signal-close, quote, detection and alert timestamps separately.

BANKNIFTY completed 5,194 recorded scan cycles with median duration one second
and 95th percentile three seconds. SENSEX has the same median and percentile;
one cycle failed with a database lock. Its zero trades chiefly reflects gate
rejections, not evidence that it never ran. Both index records end around
14:36 on October 21, so that day's later coverage should not be assumed.
Gate counts are repeated scan observations, not unique tradable opportunities.

The archive also contains broker DNS failures, database locks and candle-file
permission errors. Broker queue outliers approach 19 seconds even though typical
requests are much faster. Prioritize exit processing, reuse shared completed
candles/quotes, avoid redundant downloads, and serialize short database writes.
The personal repository is inside OneDrive; file synchronization is a possible
source of contention, not a proven cause of each lock. A local non-synced data
directory is worth testing without moving or deleting existing data blindly.

MCX context in the app packet shows one closed SILVERM winner at +1.977 modeled
net R and three open positions. The closed observation is excluded after a
monitoring interruption; open contracts also have unavailable-quote flags.
This does not support a reliable MCX win-rate estimate.
